# Sales playbook

## One-liner

*Munshi is a back-office team in your phone — it takes orders, runs the godown and the
vans, keeps the khata and chases the money — and nothing moves stock or cash unless you
or your munshi taps yes.*

## Discovery questions

- How do orders reach you today? (voice notes, calls, the salesman's notebook)
- How do you know what's in the godown right now?
- When the driver comes back, how do you check his cash against what he delivered?
- Who decides credit, and how do you know a customer is over the limit before the van leaves?
- How much is out with customers over 60 days? Who chases it, and how?
- What did the last "mistake" cost you?

## Objections and answers

**"My munshi will not use it / will resist."**
Your munshi gets the easiest job in the app: he types what he already hears, and taps
Approve. He stops re-entering, stops arguing with drivers about cash, and stops being
blamed for what he can't see. Every action has his name on it — which protects him too.

**"My data on someone else's server?"**
Your business is a separate file that only your PINs open. You can download it any time
(Settings → Backup) and take it to any other computer — it opens in any SQLite tool. Or
run it on your own PC in the office; we sell that too.

**"Internet on the route is bad."**
The driver's screen is built for that: he closes the stop, it saves on the phone, and it
sends itself when the signal comes back. He never re-enters anything.

**"I already use a khata app."**
Keep the habit, lose the app. A khata app is a ledger. Munshi *creates* the ledger entries
from deliveries and payments, so the khata is always right without anyone typing it — and
it also runs the godown, the vans and the reminders.

**"What if a phone is stolen?"**
You sign that person out from your phone in one tap. Their PIN was never on the phone.

**"Does it understand Urdu?"**
Orders in Urdu, English or both. The whole app switches to Urdu in Settings. Reminders go
to customers in Roman Urdu.

**"What does it cost?"**
Rs 4,000 a month for the office and two vans — less than a day of a clerk's salary.
It pays for itself the first time a driver's hand-in comes up short.

**"What if you disappear?"**
The code is open, the data is yours, and self-hosting is a documented one-command install.
No lock-in is the sales pitch, not a weakness.

## Closing

Free month, no card. We import your customers and products from your Excel on the
call. If the driver closes his first real stop with a customer's code in the first week,
you'll keep it.

## After the sale — the 30-day plan

Day 0: import, routes, vehicles, staff PINs, digest time. Day 1: clerk takes the day's
orders in the app; owner approves from anywhere. Day 3: first dispatch plan and driver
closes. Day 7: first reconciliation with a real shortfall attribution; first reminders.
Day 14: owner reads the profit report. Day 30: card on file; ask for two names.
