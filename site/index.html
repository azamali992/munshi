How it works

Say it. Approve it. It's done — and written down.

1 · Say it, in Urdu or English

“Chaudhry Farms ko 20 urea aur 5 dap bhej do.” The Manager sends it to the right munshi; the Order Munshi matches real products, checks stock and the khata, and drafts the order.

2 · A card asks for a yes

Every action that touches stock, money or a customer pauses as an approval card. Routine ones need the clerk; money and stock adjustments need the owner. Until someone taps, nothing has happened.

3 · The record keeps itself

Loading takes stock out. Closing a stop — with the customer’s own four-digit code — writes the invoice and the cash. The hand-in reconciles itself and names the stop to look at. The khata is never typed.

Four roles, four apps in one

Everyone gets their own screen. Nobody gets someone else’s keys.

Owner

Today at a glance: orders, deliveries, cash collected vs. handed in, receivables by age, payables, low stock. Approves money and stock. Manages staff, routes, vans, settings. Gets the 8pm digest on WhatsApp. Downloads a backup any time.

Clerk (munshi)

A desk that shows the work in order — drafts to confirm, confirmed to allocate, allocated to plan, vans to reconcile — plus chat, approvals, khata, purchases, expenses, reminders and reports.

Salesman

Book order at the counter with the customer’s balance and limit in view, log a promise to pay, check stock. Drafts wait for the office; he approves nothing and sees no cost prices.

Driver

Stops in route order with address and phone. Big fields for cash and the customer’s code. Works with no signal and syncs itself. Never sees balances or the code.

Why owners trust it

Built for the fear that keeps the phone in your hand.

Approval by construction

Gating isn’t a prompt asking the AI to be careful. Every stock- or money-changing tool pauses the agent before it runs; a person with the right role resumes it. A test suite proves un-gating one tool fails the build.

The customer holds the key

Each delivery gets a four-digit code sent to the customer. A stop cannot close without it. The driver never sees it. That’s proof of delivery you don’t have to argue about.

Every action has a name

Who asked, which munshi did it, who approved, when — in an audit log you can read and export. Nothing is ever deleted.

Your data is a file you can hold

One SQLite file per business. Download it from Settings, open it anywhere, or run the whole thing on your own PC with one command. No lock-in is the point.

Real sign-in

Phone + PIN per person, hashed, five wrong tries and you wait. Lost a phone? Sign that person out from yours in one tap.

No free text to customers

Reminders, codes, invoices and receipts are templates you approve. No AI ever composes a message to your customer.

FAQ

Questions owners ask

Will my munshi actually use it?

He gets the easiest job in the app: he types what he already hears and taps Approve. He stops re-entering, stops arguing with drivers about cash, and every action carries his name — which protects him too.

Internet on the route is bad.

The driver’s screen is built for that. He closes the stop, it saves on the phone, and it sends itself when the signal comes back. Nothing is entered twice.

Does it understand Urdu?

Orders in Urdu, English or both. The whole app switches to اردو in Settings. Reminders reach customers in Roman Urdu.

I already use a khata app.

A khata app is a ledger you type into. Munshi creates the entries from deliveries and payments, so the khata is always right — and it also runs the godown, the vans, the suppliers and the reminders.

Where is my data?

In a single file that belongs to your business. Download it any time, or run Munshi on your own PC. The code is open source under MIT.

What if a phone is stolen?

The owner signs that person out from his own phone in one tap. PINs are never stored on the phone.

What does the AI cost me?

Nothing extra. The hosted plans include it. The free plan uses a rule-based assistant that understands your catalogue and the common commands, with no AI key at all.
